## Category 1: Real Yields / Interest Rates (KR-001 to KR-018)

---

### KR-001
- Title: Real Yields Determine Gold Opportunity Cost
- Category: Real Yields
- Mechanism: Gold is a non-yielding asset. When real yields (nominal yields minus inflation expectations) rise, the opportunity cost of holding gold increases, reducing investment demand and lowering prices. When real yields fall, gold becomes more attractive.
- Preconditions: Functioning bond markets, rational investor substitution between yield-bearing and non-yielding assets.
- Trigger: Sustained move in 10-year US Treasury real yield (TIPS yield).
- Expected Gold Impact: Inverse. ~3% gold decline per 1bp increase in real yields under the historical regime (1990-2021).
- Strength: Very strong (R-squared ~85% pre-2022).
- Confidence: High (pre-2022 historical regime). Low (post-2022 regime where R-squared dropped to 16%).
- Historical Evidence: 1990-2021 monthly data: 10-year US TIPS yield explains ~85% of variation in spot gold prices. Relationship held across multiple Fed cycles. Gold topped in 2011 as real yields bottomed; gold bottomed in 2015 as real yields peaked.
- Counter Examples: 2022-present: gold rose while real yields rose sharply. The statistical relationship broke down. Correlation flipped from negative to flat/positive.
- Regime Dependence: Dominant in Normal Growth regime. Weakens in Inflationary regime. Irrelevant in acute Liquidity Crisis regime.
- Failure Conditions: Regime change where a new driver (fiscal credibility, term premium, geopolitical risk) overwhelms the opportunity cost mechanism. Central bank buying creating a structural price floor independent of yields.
- References: BlackRock CMA framework, J.P. Morgan "Is Gold's Old Playbook Broken?" (2026), World Gold Council GRAM methodology.

### KR-002
- Title: Gold Sensitivity to Real Yields Is Regime-Dependent in Magnitude
- Category: Real Yields
- Mechanism: The beta of gold to real yields changes across macro regimes and estimation windows. Short-window estimates show higher sensitivity than long-window averages during periods when rates are the market's focus.
- Preconditions: A functioning macro environment where real yields are the marginal driver.
- Trigger: Central bank policy focus shifting (e.g., Fed explicitly targeting inflation vs employment).
- Expected Gold Impact: Coefficient varies. In high-focus periods (rates front and center), gold sensitivity to rates rises ~3x versus long-run average.
- Strength: Strong for short-window estimation, moderate for long-window.
- Confidence: Medium. Coefficient instability is well-documented but difficult to predict ex ante.
- Historical Evidence: WGC GRAM weekly model: in the past year gold's sensitivity to interest rates rose three-fold compared to the full 14-year period. Rolling windows show sensitivity spikes during rate-focused market environments.
- Counter Examples: Periods where gold and rates move together (2022-2023) show the coefficient can temporarily flip sign.
- Regime Dependence: Sensitivity is highest when inflation is uppermost in investors' minds. WGC: "gold's sensitivity to rates tends to rise when the market is paying more attention to what central banks are doing."
- Failure Conditions: Regime change where rates cease to be the marginal driver (e.g., central bank buying dominates).
- References: World Gold Council short-term gold performance model (2021), GRAM methodology.

### KR-003
- Title: Real Yield Breakdown in 2022: Structural Regime Change
- Category: Real Yields
- Mechanism: The historical gold-real yield relationship broke down in 2022. Gold prices proved far more resilient than the historical regression would predict. Beta flipped from negative to positive. R-squared declined from ~85% to ~16%.
- Preconditions: Post-COVID inflation surge, Russia-Ukraine invasion, sanctions regime change, fiscal expansion.
- Trigger: Russia-Ukraine war February 2022 and subsequent financial sanctions.
- Expected Gold Impact: Gold ceased to respond to real yields as the historical model predicted.
- Strength: Regime break is statistically unambiguous. R-squared drop from 85% to 16% is a structural change.
- Confidence: Very high that the relationship changed. Low-to-medium on what specifically replaced it.
- Historical Evidence: J.P. Morgan regression analysis: 1990-2021 R-squared 85%. 2022 onward R-squared 16%. The relationship did not gradually weaken; it collapsed abruptly in 2022.
- Counter Examples: Brief periods of relationship reassertion in 2023-2024 suggest the old relationship can temporarily return.
- Regime Dependence: The breakdown is specific to the post-2022 Inflationary/Geopolitical regime.
- Failure Conditions: Return to a Normal Growth regime where real yields regain dominance.
- References: J.P. Morgan Asset Management "Is Gold's Old Playbook Broken?" (April 2026), J.P. Morgan Private Bank "Is it a golden era for gold?" (Feb 2026).

### KR-004
- Title: Term Premium Replaced Real Yields as Gold's Primary Driver Post-2022
- Category: Real Yields
- Mechanism: Gold's relationship with the 10-year term premium (compensation investors demand for holding long-duration government bonds) became stronger than its relationship with real yields post-2022. Gold is increasingly reacting not to the level of real rates but to concerns about fiscal credibility, policy uncertainty, and long-term macro risk.
- Preconditions: Fiscal sustainability concerns, central bank independence questions, elevated government debt.
- Trigger: Post-2022 macro environment.
- Expected Gold Impact: Positive correlation with term premium. As term premium rises (fiscal/credibility concerns increase), gold rises.
- Strength: Strong. R-squared of gold vs term premium now exceeds gold vs real yields across US, Eurozone, UK, and Japan.
- Confidence: Medium-high. Multiple independent analyses confirm the shift. However, the relationship still has significant unexplained variance.
- Historical Evidence: J.P. Morgan: repeating the regression analysis using term premium as the independent variable instead of real yields produces far stronger R-squared post-2022. Rolling three-year correlations flipped from negative to positive starting 2020.
- Counter Examples: 2002-2020: gold and term premium were generally negatively correlated. The shift since 2020 is historically unusual.
- Regime Dependence: Dominant in Inflationary and Stagflationary regimes where fiscal credibility is the primary concern.
- Failure Conditions: Return to fiscal credibility (debt reduction, central bank independence reaffirmed) where term premium compresses and real yields regain dominance.
- References: J.P. Morgan Asset Management "Is Gold's Old Playbook Broken?" (April 2026).

### KR-005
- Title: Negative Real Rates Strongly Support Gold
- Category: Real Yields
- Mechanism: When real interest rates are negative, the opportunity cost of holding gold disappears or becomes a benefit. Gold preserves purchasing power while yield-bearing assets guarantee a loss of real purchasing power. This creates a structural bid.
- Preconditions: Central bank policy setting nominal rates below inflation.
- Trigger: Real yields crossing below zero.
- Expected Gold Impact: Strongly positive. Gold tends to appreciate or maintain value as investors seek non-yielding stores of value.
- Strength: Very strong historically.
- Confidence: High.
- Historical Evidence: 1970s negative real rates period: gold rallied from ~$35 to ~$850. 2001-2011: negative real rates supported gold's bull market from ~$270 to ~$1900. 2020-2021: deeply negative real rates coincided with gold's rally to ~$2075.
- Counter Examples: 2022: negative real rates did not prevent gold from initially falling as the dollar rallied and liquidity tightened.
- Regime Dependence: Most powerful in Stagflationary regime. Moderate in Normal regime.
- Failure Conditions: Liquidity crisis where all assets are sold for dollars regardless of real rate environment.
- References: Bridgewater All Weather framework, Dalio "A Template for Understanding How the Economic Machine Works."

### KR-006
- Title: Rate Cut Cycles Historically Support Gold
- Category: Real Yields
- Mechanism: When the Fed begins cutting rates, gold tends to rally. Lower nominal rates reduce the opportunity cost of holding gold and typically signal a macro environment (slowing growth, falling inflation) that is supportive for gold.
- Preconditions: Fed cutting cycle commenced.
- Trigger: First rate cut in the cycle.
- Expected Gold Impact: Positive. Average ~11% in the six months following the first cut.
- Strength: Strong.
- Confidence: High. 80% win rate across 10 cutting cycles.
- Historical Evidence: Over the past ten Fed rate cutting cycles, gold has risen over the subsequent six months 80% of the time by an average of 11%.
- Counter Examples: 2001-2003 cutting cycle: gold initially fell before rallying. The relationship is not mechanical.
- Regime Dependence: Most reliable when cuts are driven by growth concerns (not inflation).
- Failure Conditions: Cutting cycle driven by inflationary recession where the Fed cuts into inflation.
- References: J.P. Morgan Asset Management "The Economics Behind a Modern-Day Gold Rush" (2025).

### KR-007
- Title: Real Yields and Gold ETF Flows Are Linked
- Category: Real Yields
- Mechanism: Changes in real yields drive investor allocation decisions into and out of gold ETFs. Rising real yields trigger ETF outflows; falling real yields trigger inflows. This is the transmission mechanism from macro yields to gold prices via investor flows.
- Preconditions: Active gold ETF market (GLD, IAUM, etc.), yield-sensitive investor base.
- Trigger: Sustained real yield move of 25bp+.
- Expected Gold Impact: Amplified by ETF flow channel. Real yield changes affect gold directly (opportunity cost) and indirectly (ETF flows).
- Strength: Strong.
- Confidence: Medium-high.
- Historical Evidence: Post-2022: J.P. Morgan reduced its ETF inflow forecast from 580t to 400t based on the real yield environment. The 2020 gold rally was accompanied by record ETF inflows as real yields went deeply negative.
- Counter Examples: 2025: gold rallied to $4,000+ even as ETF flows were sometimes negative, indicating the marginal buyer shifted from ETFs to central banks.
- Regime Dependence: Strongest in Normal Growth regime. Weaker in Central Bank-Driven regime.
- Failure Conditions: ETF market share declines relative to other gold investment channels.
- References: J.P. Morgan gold research, WGC Gold Demand Trends.

### KR-008
- Title: Real Yields and DXY Co-Determine Gold in Normal Regimes
- Category: Real Yields
- Mechanism: Gold is priced in USD and non-yielding. Both the level of real yields (USD-denominated) and the dollar itself are independent inputs to gold's fair value. In normal regimes, changes in real yields and DXY tend to be correlated (higher rates attract capital, strengthening USD), reinforcing gold's move.
- Preconditions: Normal macro regime, integrated capital markets.
- Trigger: Simultaneous move in real yields and DXY.
- Expected Gold Impact: Amplified inverse. Higher real yields + stronger USD = stronger headwind for gold than either alone.
- Strength: Very strong when both move in the same direction.
- Confidence: High.
- Historical Evidence: 2013 Taper Tantrum: real yields rose + DXY rose, gold fell ~28%. 2014-2015: real yields stable but DXY rose sharply, gold fell. 2018: both rose, gold fell to ~$1,180.
- Counter Examples: 2020: both real yields and DXY fell, gold rallied ~25%. 2022: both rose initially, gold fell but then recovered as the relationship broke.
- Regime Dependence: Most reliable in Normal Growth regime. Becomes unreliable when the real yield-gold relationship breaks.
- Failure Conditions: As per KR-003.
- References: Bridgewater All Weather framework, Goldman Sachs commodity research.

### KR-009
- Title: Short-Term Real Yield Changes Have Asymmetric Impact on Gold
- Category: Real Yields
- Mechanism: Gold responds asymmetrically to real yield changes. The historical relationship shows that gold's sensitivity to real yields is not linear. Gold falls less when real yields rise than it rises when real yields fall, due to structural gold demand sources (central banks, jewelry) that are price-inelastic at certain levels.
- Preconditions: Large real yield moves.
- Trigger: Real yield change >1 standard deviation.
- Expected Gold Impact: Larger impact from falling yields than from rising yields in the pre-2022 regime.
- Strength: Moderate asymmetry.
- Confidence: Medium.
- Historical Evidence: Pre-2022 data: gold rallies of 5-10% on 50bp yield declines vs declines of 3-7% on 50bp yield increases. The asymmetry suggests structural buyers (central banks, long-term holders) provide a floor.
- Counter Examples: 2013: real yields rose 100bp, gold fell 28%—asymmetric but in the opposite direction (larger decline than would be predicted).
- Regime Dependence: Asymmetry is regime-dependent. It strengthens when central bank buying is active.
- Failure Conditions: Central bank buying ceases during a yield spike. COT longs are forced to liquidate.
- References: J.P. Morgan gold research, World Gold Council GRAM model.

### KR-010
- Title: Long-End Yield Curve Shape Matters for Gold
- Category: Real Yields
- Mechanism: The slope of the yield curve (2y10y spread) affects gold through the banking and economic cycle channel. A flat/inverted curve signals impending recession, which historically leads to Fed easing and lower real yields, supporting gold. A steepening curve signals growth expectations, which can be bullish or bearish for gold depending on whether it reflects real growth or inflation expectations.
- Preconditions: Functioning yield curve, recession-forecasting power.
- Trigger: Curve inversion below zero, curve steepening above +100bp.
- Expected Gold Impact: Curve inversion = medium-term bullish for gold (recession → rate cuts → lower real yields). Curve steepening from inflation = bullish for gold (rising breakevens). Curve steepening from growth = neutral/bearish (higher real yields).
- Strength: Moderate.
- Confidence: Medium.
- Historical Evidence: 2000 inversion → gold rally 2001-2008. 2006-2007 inversion → gold peak in 2011. 2019 inversion → gold rally to $2,075 in 2020. 2022-2023 inversion → gold rally.
- Counter Examples: 1998 inversion → gold continued to decline until 2001. The relationship has timing uncertainty.
- Regime Dependence: Stronger in Normal Growth/Transition regimes. Weaker in Inflationary regime where curve steepening reflects inflation.
- Failure Conditions: Yield curve signal becomes noise due to central bank quantitative easing distorting term structure.
- References: Bridgewater business cycle template, Fed yield curve research.

### KR-011
- Title: Global Real Yield Correlation Affects Gold's Diversification Value
- Category: Real Yields
- Mechanism: Gold's diversification benefit depends on its correlation with bonds and equities. When real yields are the common driver across asset classes, gold's non-yielding nature provides genuine diversification. When real yields lose explanatory power and gold correlates with risk assets, its diversification benefit erodes.
- Preconditions: Global real yield correlation regime.
- Trigger: Regime change in gold's correlation structure.
- Expected Gold Impact: Indirect. Affects gold's strategic allocation value, not its short-term price direction.
- Strength: Moderate for portfolio construction.
- Confidence: Medium.
- Historical Evidence: 1990-2021: gold's negative correlation with real yields provided portfolio diversification. 2022 onward: gold and equities have sometimes risen together, reducing diversification utility.
- Counter Examples: Periods where gold's diversification benefit reasserts even within the new regime.
- Regime Dependence: Regime-dependent by definition.
- Failure Conditions: Gold permanently loses its negative correlation with real yields.
- References: J.P. Morgan Asset Management "Is Gold's Old Playbook Broken?" (2026).

### KR-012
- Title: Breakeven Inflation Rate Separates Nominal and Real Effects for Gold
- Category: Real Yields
- Mechanism: The breakeven inflation rate (BEI) = nominal yield minus real yield. Gold is sensitive to both the real yield component (opportunity cost) and the inflation expectations component (hedge demand). Changes in BEI allow the analyst to decompose gold's reaction into rate-driven and inflation-driven components. Rising BEI = inflation expectations rising, supportive for gold.
- Preconditions: Liquid TIPS and nominal Treasury markets.
- Trigger: BEI move of 10bp+ in a month.
- Expected Gold Impact: Rising BEI supports gold through the inflation hedge channel. Falling BEI pressures gold.
- Strength: Strong. WGC GRAM: breakeven inflation rate has had one of the largest absolute impacts on gold returns since 2007.
- Confidence: Medium-high.
- Historical Evidence: WGC GRAM full-period model: breakeven inflation rate (along with interest rates) are the largest absolute drivers of gold returns since 2007.
- Counter Examples: 2022: BEI rose sharply but gold did not rally initially (USD and real yields dominated).
- Regime Dependence: Most powerful in Inflationary regime. Less powerful in Deflationary/Crisis regime.
- Failure Conditions: BEI becomes a poor proxy for actual inflation expectations due to TIPS market dislocations.
- References: World Gold Council short-term gold performance model (2021), GRAM methodology.

### KR-013
- Title: Gold Historically Peaked ~6 Months After Last Fed Hike
- Category: Real Yields
- Mechanism: In previous cycles, gold's bull market peak occurred approximately six months after the Fed's final rate hike in the tightening cycle. Peak gold coincides with the end of the tightening cycle when real yields are at their maximum and the market begins pricing cuts.
- Preconditions: Identifiable final Fed hike of the cycle.
- Trigger: Last rate hike confirmed (forward guidance, data).
- Expected Gold Impact: Gold resumes rallying post-hike peak.
- Strength: Moderate timing signal.
- Confidence: Medium.
- Historical Evidence: 2006 last hike → gold peaked 2008. 2018 last hike → gold peaked 2020 (18 months later, COVID timing). 2000 last hike → gold bottomed 2001 and began multi-year rally.
- Counter Examples: Not every cycle shows this pattern clearly. 2023-2024: gold rallied before the hiking cycle was clearly over.
- Regime Dependence: More reliable in Normal regime where rate cycles are the primary driver.
- Failure Conditions: Rate cycle is not the dominant gold driver. Central bank buying or geopolitical risk overrides the rate cycle signal.
- References: J.P. Morgan gold research, Goldman Sachs commodity research.

### KR-014
- Title: Gold Real Yield Sensitivity Increases When Inflation Is Central in Investor Thinking
- Category: Real Yields
- Mechanism: The sensitivity of gold to interest rates tends to rise when the market is paying more attention to central bank policy, which is often the case when inflation is uppermost in investors' minds. During these periods, gold's daily and weekly reactions to rate moves are amplified.
- Preconditions: Inflation-focused market environment.
- Trigger: Periods of elevated inflation or central bank rate changes.
- Expected Gold Impact: Gold responds more sharply to rate news than in normal periods.
- Strength: Moderate-to-strong amplification effect.
- Confidence: Medium (WGC finding).
- Historical Evidence: WGC GRAM rolling regression: gold's sensitivity to rates and inflation expectations are positively correlated—when one increases, the other also tends to increase. The effect is visible in both monthly and weekly windows.
- Counter Examples: 2015-2019: low inflation period, gold's rate sensitivity was muted.
- Regime Dependence: Directly regime-dependent.
- Failure Conditions: Central bank loses credibility, rate changes no longer transmit to gold.
- References: World Gold Council short-term gold performance model (2021).

### KR-015
- Title: Zero Lower Bound Eliminates Real Yield Opportunity Cost
- Category: Real Yields
- Mechanism: When nominal rates are at zero (ZLB), the opportunity cost of holding gold is minimized regardless of inflation. Even if real yields are negative, the ZLB prevents them from being "more negative." This creates an asymmetric environment where gold can only gain (from a yield perspective) as rates cannot go lower but can eventually rise.
- Preconditions: Central bank policy rate at or near zero.
- Trigger: Rates at ZLB.
- Expected Gold Impact: Structurally supportive for gold.
- Strength: Moderate.
- Confidence: Medium-high.
- Historical Evidence: 2010-2015 ZLB period: gold rallied to ~$1,900 and maintained elevated levels. 2020-2021 ZLB: gold rallied to record highs. Japan's multi-decade ZLB: gold in yen terms has been in a secular bull market.
- Counter Examples: 2022: rates rose from ZLB and gold initially fell but then recovered.
- Regime Dependence: Applies to any regime where ZLB is in effect.
- Failure Conditions: Rates rise from ZLB and the transition period creates gold headwinds.
- References: Bridgewater debt cycle framework, Dalio "How the Economic Machine Works."

### KR-016
- Title: Real Yield Volatility Pressures Gold More Than Real Yield Level
- Category: Real Yields
- Mechanism: Gold is more sensitive to the volatility of real yields than to their absolute level. Rapid changes in real yields create uncertainty about the macro outlook, which affects gold through both the opportunity cost channel and the risk-pricing channel. High yield volatility often coincides with regime transitions that are supportive for gold.
- Preconditions: Bond market volatility elevated.
- Trigger: MOVE index spike, large daily yield swings.
- Expected Gold Impact: Mixed. Initial volatility spike can pressure gold (liquidation for margin), but sustained volatility supports gold (uncertainty premium).
- Strength: Moderate.
- Confidence: Medium.
- Historical Evidence: 2013 Taper Tantrum: real yield volatility spiked, gold fell sharply short-term but stabilized as volatility persisted. 2020 COVID: yield volatility spiked, gold initially fell then rallied to record highs.
- Counter Examples: 2022 gold fell despite elevated yield volatility.
- Regime Dependence: The direction of gold's response depends on whether the volatility is driven by growth concerns (gold positive) or inflation concerns (gold initially negative, then positive).
- Failure Conditions: Volatility overwhelms all fundamental signals and the market becomes purely flow-driven.
- References: BlackRock risk models, Bridgewater risk parity framework.

### KR-017
- Title: Gold's Inverse Relationship with Real Yields Weakens When Fiscal Dominance Operates
- Category: Real Yields
- Mechanism: When the fiscal authority dominates over the monetary authority (central bank accommodates fiscal expansion by keeping rates artificially low), real yields are suppressed regardless of inflation or growth. The traditional inverse relationship between real yields and gold weakens because both lose their fundamental driver: real yields are no longer set by market forces, and gold reflects fiscal risk rather than opportunity cost.
- Preconditions: Fiscal dominance regime (central bank indirect financing of government debt).
- Trigger: Large fiscal deficits combined with central bank accommodation.
- Expected Gold Impact: Strongly positive. Gold rises as a hedge against fiscal debasement regardless of real yield level.
- Strength: Strong.
- Confidence: Medium-high.
- Historical Evidence: 2020-2021: US fiscal deficits of 15% of GDP, Fed accommodating, gold at all-time highs despite low (but not zero) real yields. COVID era: gold rose as central banks monetized fiscal deficits globally.
- Counter Examples: Japan: fiscal dominance for decades but gold in USD terms did not have a sustained bull market until the 2000s.
- Regime Dependence: Operates in Fiscal Dominance regime.
- Failure Conditions: Return to Monetary Dominance regime where central bank independence is restored.
- References: Bridgewater long-term debt cycle, Dalio "Principles for Navigating Big Debt Crises," LSE research on fiscal dominance.

### KR-018
- Title: Gold Real Yield Beta Is Higher in Small Open Economies
- Category: Real Yields
- Mechanism: Gold's sensitivity to US real yields is higher when measured in non-USD currencies, particularly in emerging market currencies. This is because the gold price in local currency reflects both the USD gold price and the local currency's sensitivity to US rates. A rise in US real yields strengthens the USD and weakens EM currencies, creating a double impact on local currency gold prices.
- Preconditions: Non-USD local currency investor base.
- Trigger: US real yield changes.
- Expected Gold Impact: Amplified in EM local currency terms. A 1% gold decline in USD becomes a 2-3% decline in TRY, INR, or BRL.
- Strength: Strong amplification.
- Confidence: High.
- Historical Evidence: 2013 Taper Tantrum: gold fell ~28% in USD but 45%+ in INR and TRY due to currency depreciation. 2022: gold was flat in USD but up 20%+ in JPY and EUR.
- Counter Examples: 2020-2021: gold rallied in all currencies, but the EM amplification was less pronounced as EM currencies also rallied.
- Regime Dependence: Persistent across all regimes where US rates affect EM FX.
- Failure Conditions: USD weakens during US rate hikes (unusual but possible in a Fed cutting cycle).
- References: IMF gold in reserves research, BIS working papers on reserve management.

### KR-019
- Title: USD and Gold Have Strong Inverse Relationship
- Category: USD / FX
- Mechanism: Gold is priced in US dollars. When the dollar strengthens against major currencies, gold becomes more expensive for non-USD holders, reducing demand. Conversely, a weaker dollar makes gold cheaper for the majority of global buyers, increasing demand. The relationship also reflects the dollar's role as a reserve currency and gold as the alternative reserve asset.
- Preconditions: USD-denominated gold pricing, global gold market.
- Trigger: Sustained DXY move of 2%+.
- Expected Gold Impact: Inverse. DXY up 2% = gold down ~1-2% (not 1:1 due to other drivers).
- Strength: Strong. DXY is consistently one of gold's top 3 drivers.
- Confidence: High.
- Historical Evidence: 2014-2015: DXY rose ~25%, gold fell ~40%. 2017-2018: DXY fell ~10%, gold rose ~15%. 2020: DXY fell ~10%, gold rose ~25%. 2021-2022: DXY rose ~20%, gold initially fell then recovered.
- Counter Examples: 2023-2025: gold and DXY occasionally moved together (both up), indicating other drivers (central bank buying, geopolitical risk) overwhelmed the inverse relationship.
- Regime Dependence: Strongest in Normal regime. Weaker when gold-specific drivers (CB buying) dominate.
- Failure Conditions: Gold demand becomes driven primarily by non-USD buyers (central banks who do not convert USD to local currency).
- References: Bridgewater exchange rate framework, J.P. Morgan FX research, WGC GRAM methodology.

### KR-020
- Title: USD Reserve Currency Status Is a Gold Driver
- Category: USD / FX
- Mechanism: Concerns about the USD's reserve currency status drive central banks and sovereign wealth funds to diversify into gold. When the dollar's credibility is questioned (fiscal concerns, sanctions weaponization, inflation), gold benefits as the primary alternative reserve asset. This is a structural, multi-year driver.
- Preconditions: USD as dominant reserve currency facing challenges.
- Trigger: US fiscal deterioration, sanctions policy, competitor currency emergence.
- Expected Gold Impact: Positive structural support over multi-year horizon.
- Strength: Strong for long-term gold direction. Difficult to measure short-term.
- Confidence: Medium-high. IMF research confirms sanctions drive gold buying.
- Historical Evidence: Post-2022 sanctions on Russia triggered accelerated gold buying by non-G7 central banks. USD share of global reserves declined from ~70% (2000) to ~58% (2024). IMF research: sanctions imposition causally increases gold share of reserves.
- Counter Examples: USD reserve status has been questioned for two decades but the dollar remains dominant (>58% of allocated reserves). The decline has been gradual, not sudden.
- Regime Dependence: Active in Geopolitical Stress regime. Structural in all regimes.
- Failure Conditions: USD regains reserve share through policy credibility or absence of alternatives.
- References: IMF "Gold as a Barbarous Relic No More?" (2023), Fed "De-Dollarization? Diversification?" IFDP paper.

### KR-021
- Title: Gold-Dollar Correlation Varies by Currency Pair
- Category: USD / FX
- Mechanism: The relationship between gold and individual currency pairs varies significantly. Gold has a strong negative correlation with USD/JPY and EUR/USD but a weaker negative (or even positive) correlation with commodity currencies (AUD, CAD, NZD). The WGC GRAM model uses equal-weighted currency indices (EUR, JPY, CNY, AUD) rather than DXY to capture this variation.
- Preconditions: Multiple currency pairs.
- Trigger: Currency-specific moves that diverge from DXY.
- Expected Gold Impact: Varies by currency. Gold responds more to DM currency moves than EM currency moves.
- Strength: Moderate-to-strong depending on the currency.
- Confidence: Medium.
- Historical Evidence: WGC GRAM: gold's sensitivity to DM currencies increased nearly four-fold over the last three years compared to the full 14-year period. The DXY index, which is 57% EUR, does not capture gold's full FX exposure.
- Counter Examples: Periods where all currencies move together against gold.
- Regime Dependence: Currency-sensitivity increases when FX is the marginal gold driver (usually during USD bull/bear trends).
- Failure Conditions: All currency correlations converge to 1 during a global shock (e.g., COVID March 2020).
- References: World Gold Council GRAM methodology, WGC short-term gold performance model.

### KR-022
- Title: Dollar Liquidity Conditions Drive Gold More Than Dollar Level
- Category: USD / FX
- Mechanism: The availability of dollar liquidity (swap lines, FIMA repo, FX swap basis, central bank dollar reserves) is a stronger gold driver than the dollar's level. Dollar scarcity forces liquidation of all assets including gold to meet funding needs. Dollar abundance supports gold as liquidity flows into assets.
- Preconditions: Global dollar funding market stress.
- Trigger: FX swap basis widening, FRA-OIS spike, Libor-OIS widening.
- Expected Gold Impact: Dollar scarcity = gold down sharply. Dollar abundance = gold up sharply.
- Strength: Very strong during stress periods.
- Confidence: High.
- Historical Evidence: March 2020: dollar liquidity crisis, gold fell ~12% in days. March 2020 Fed swap lines established, gold recovered + rallied. 2008: dollar scarcity, gold fell from ~$1,000 to ~$680. Late 2008: dollar abundance restored, gold rallied.
- Counter Examples: 2022: dollar strengthening did not create the same liquidity crisis gold decline, as central bank buying provided a price floor.
- Regime Dependence: Dominant in Crisis/Deflationary regime. Less relevant in Normal regime.
- Failure Conditions: Dollar liquidity ceases to be the binding constraint in a crisis (e.g., if gold is accepted as collateral for dollar funding).
- References: Fed swap line history, BIS dollar funding research, IMF gold liquidity analysis.

### KR-023
- Title: De-Dollarization Narrative Is Concentrated in Few Countries
- Category: USD / FX
- Mechanism: Despite the aggregate narrative of global de-dollarization, gold-linked reserve diversification is concentrated in a small number of countries. The IMF identifies 14 "active diversifiers" that raised gold's share of reserves by 5+ percentage points since 2000. Outside China, Russia, and Turkey, most gold-buying countries have not de-dollarized their reserves.
- Preconditions: Large USD reserve holdings, geopolitical motivation.
- Trigger: Specific country-level decision.
- Expected Gold Impact: Structural but concentrated demand. Not a uniform global driver.
- Strength: Strong for the specific countries, weak for the aggregate narrative.
- Confidence: High (IMF empirical finding).
- Historical Evidence: IMF regression analysis: outside China and Russia, gold purchases are not associated with USD selling. Most countries accumulate gold alongside USD reserves rather than replacing USD.
- Counter Examples: Russia: sold nearly all USD reserves and accumulated 2,000t of gold. China: reduced USD share while accumulating gold.
- Regime Dependence: Geopolitical/Structural regime.
- Failure Conditions: Sanctions regime change or return to multilateral reserve coordination.
- References: IMF WP/23/008 (Arslanalp, Eichengreen, Simpson-Bell 2023), Fed IFDP 1420.

### KR-024
- Title: Gold Outperforms in USD Bear Markets
- Category: USD / FX
- Mechanism: During prolonged USD bear markets, gold tends to be one of the best-performing asset classes. The inverse relationship is amplified because a weaker dollar benefits gold through both the pricing mechanism and the reserve diversification motive simultaneously.
